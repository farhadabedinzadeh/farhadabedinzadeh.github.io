01 / Current research

Understanding change.
Predicting earlier.

Connecting retinal images, ocular measurements and information collected over time.

PhD · Durham University

Interpretable AI for early prediction of childhood myopia

My PhD investigates longitudinal, multimodal retinal imaging and biometric data, with the aim of identifying changes that may precede the onset of myopia in children.

I am interested in models that capture how an eye changes over time, combine complementary measurements and offer explanations that can be assessed against clinical knowledge.

Retinal imagingOcular biometryInterpretable prediction
01

Imaging the retina

OCT, OCTA and en face imaging to explore retinal structure and vascular features.

02

Measuring the eye

Axial length and corneal measurements alongside repeated observations of ocular development.

03

Learning over time

Multimodal and longitudinal modelling, with interpretability and clinically relevant evaluation.

Imaging

From pixels to biomarkers

Retinal segmentation, thickness mapping, foveal localisation and quantitative OCT/OCTA analysis.

Modelling

From observations to prediction

Deep learning, multimodal integration, foundation models and learning from longitudinal data.

Interpretation

From outputs to understanding

Interpretable AI, reproducible analysis and careful evaluation of biomedical models.

Research foundations

My previous work spans biomedical image and signal processing, computational neuroscience and machine learning, including EEG connectivity, EMG, gait, voice and MRI analysis in neurological disorders. That experience now informs my work in ophthalmic AI.
